TL; DR

Given an array of `n` positive integers, choose 2 (or the only 1) of the first 3 elements and remove them with the cost equal to the higher one removed.
Repeat until everything is removed. 
Return the lowest possible total cost.

---

## Problem Statement

Alice is running her doll stall at a bustling flea market. To maximize her earnings, she’s offering a special deal: "Buy one, get one free". But, only three dolls are displayed at a time, and customers can choose two of these to buy together. The cost of the transaction will always be equal to the higher-priced doll in the pair.

Each time a purchase is made, Alice restocks the stall by adding the next 2 (all if less than 2 remains) dolls from her collection in order, repeating this process until all dolls are sold.
If there is only one doll remaining, the last customer can not get another free doll.

How much can Alice earn at least from her entire collection?

## Input

- `arr` — a list of `n` positive integers where `arr[i]` represents the price of the `i`-th doll.

## Constraints

- `0 <= n < 10^5`
- `1 <= arr[i] < 2^31`

## Output

- A single integer representing the minimum total amount Alice can earn from selling all her dolls.

## Example 

### Input 1

```sh
[1, 10, 100, 50]
```

### Output 1

```sh
110
```

### Explanation 1

One possible selling sequence is:

- Alice sells the dolls with prices 1 and 10 together (earning 10).
- Alice sells the dolls with prices 100 and 50 together (earning 100).

The total earnings are 10 + 100 = 110.

### Input 2

```sh
[1, 10, 100, 5]
```

### Output 2

```sh
105
```

### Explanation 2

One possible selling sequence is:

- Alice sells the dolls with prices 1 and 10 together (earning 10).
- Alice sells the dolls with prices 100 and 5 together (earning 100).

The total earnings are 10 + 100 = 110.

However, a less profitable sequence exists:
- Alice sells the dolls with prices 10 and 100 together (earning 100).
- Alice sells the dolls with prices 1 and 5 together (earning 5).

The total earnings are 5 + 100 = 105.

### Input 3

```sh
[9, 5, 7, 8, 6]
```

### Output 3

```sh
22
```

### Explanation 3

One possible selling sequence is:

- Alice sells the dolls with prices 5 and 7 together (earning 7).
- Alice sells the dolls with prices 9 and 8 together (earning 9).
- Finally, the last doll with price 6 remains, and Alice earns 6 from selling it alone.

The total earnings are 7 + 9 + 6 = 22.

Any other selling sequences have earnings not less than 22, thus return 22.
